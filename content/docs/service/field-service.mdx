# Field Service

A **case** is a conversation. Some repairs need somebody to physically show up —
that is a **work order**: one engineer, one site, one appointment.

Field Service adds two records to HotCRM and one board to work them from:

| Record | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| 🖥️ **Asset** | A unit of equipment installed at a customer site — which customer, which address, which model, which serial number, and when its warranty ends. |
| 🔧 **Work Order** | A visit to that unit — who goes, when, what they did, who signed for it, and whether it is chargeable. |

## Assets — your installed base

Products in the catalogue are **models**. An asset is a **machine**: the actual
unit sitting on a customer's factory floor.

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Account** / **Site Contact** | Who owns it, and who to call before an engineer arrives |
| **Product / Model**, **Serial Number** | What it is — the serial is what a customer reads out over the phone, so it is searchable from the global search box |
| **Site Address** / **Site Location** | Where the engineer actually drives to; the location also drives the **Map** view |
| **Install Date** | When it went in |
| **Warranty Start / End** | The coverage window — this is what decides whether a visit is free |
| **Under Warranty** | Kept current for you (see below) |
| **Service Contract** | Extended coverage beyond the factory warranty |

### The warranty shortcut

Record an install date and leave the warranty dates blank, and HotCRM fills in a
**12-month** factory warranty starting from the install date. Override either
date whenever the real coverage differs.

A daily sweep clears the **Under Warranty** flag on assets whose coverage has
lapsed, and notifies the asset owner — so a warranty running out never goes
unnoticed just because nobody happened to open the record.

### Asset views

- **Fleet Status** — every unit grouped by state: installed, under repair,
  suspended, decommissioned.
- **Map** — where the machines physically are.
- **Warranty Expiry Calendar** — expiries laid out by date, so renewal
  conversations happen before the coverage lapses, not after.
- **Out of Warranty** — the chargeable installed base.
- **Under Repair** — what is down right now.

## Work orders

### Raising one from a case

The support desk takes the call. On any open case, **Create Work Order** carries
the account, the contact and the reported problem onto a new work order and
links the two records. The agent supplies only what the case cannot know: the
**work type** and the **field priority**.

The case then moves to *Waiting on Support* — it stays open until the visit
lands, so the caller is never stranded.

### Response promises

Every work order gets a **Response Due** deadline the moment it is saved:

| Priority | Response promised within |
| --- | --- |
| 🔴 **Critical** | 4 hours |
| 🟠 **High** | 8 hours |
| 🟡 **Medium** | 24 hours (same day) |
| ⚪ **Low** | 72 hours |

- Escalating a job to a higher priority **pulls its deadline in** from the
  moment of the escalation.
- A deadline you type in by hand always wins — use it when you have promised the
  customer a specific time.
- The promise is that somebody **responds**, not that the repair is finished:
  the clock stops the moment the work order leaves *New*.

An **hourly** sweep flags any unfinished work order whose deadline passed with
nobody responding, and alerts the **service supervisor** — the work order's
owner. The sweep never rewrites the job's status; scheduling stays the
dispatcher's call.

### The 8 work-order statuses

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| ⚪ **New** | Raised, nobody assigned yet *(default)* |
| 🔵 **Scheduled** | An appointment window exists |
| 🟣 **Dispatched** | An engineer is on the way |
| 🟠 **In Progress** | Work is happening on site |
| 🟡 **On Hold** | Blocked — waiting on a part, or on the customer |
| 🟢 **Completed** | The visit is done and signed for |
| 🟢 **Closed** | Billing and the call-back are done too |
| ⚫ **Cancelled** | Not going ahead |

A work order stays on the open queues while it is *Completed* — it still owes a
charge and a satisfaction rating.

## Dispatch

Assign a **Field Engineer** and a **Scheduled Start / End**, and the visit shows
up on two boards:

- **Dispatch Calendar** — every scheduled visit, coloured by urgency. This is
  what dispatch works from.
- **Engineer Schedule** — the same visits as one lane *per engineer*, which is
  what answers "who is free on Thursday, and how many calls does this person
  already have that day".

Schedule someone into a window that overlaps one of their own visits and the
work order is flagged **Schedule Conflict**. It is a warning, not a block —
dispatch sometimes stacks two jobs on one industrial park deliberately.

Two queues make the board workable:

- **Awaiting Dispatch** — nobody assigned yet, urgent first.
- **Response at Risk** — open, unanswered, soonest deadline first.

## Free or chargeable — settled before you dispatch

When a work order names an asset, HotCRM reads that asset's warranty **on save**
and stamps the work order with:

- **Warranty Status** — *Under Warranty*, *Out of Warranty*, or *Unknown*
- **Billable Visit** — checked whenever the asset is out of warranty

Because it happens on save, the charge is visible on the record *before* anyone
is sent — which is the point. The **Billable Visits** queue lists every
chargeable job so nothing goes out without the customer being told.

Once a work order is closed or cancelled, its billing decision is **frozen**: a
warranty expiring later cannot rewrite the terms of a job that already happened.

## Completing the visit

**Complete Visit** on the work order collects everything from one form:

| What | Required? |
| --- | --- |
| **Work Performed** | Yes — a completion without it is rejected |
| **Accepted By** (the name of the person at the site) | Yes |
| **Parts Used**, **Labor Hours**, **Service Charge** | Optional |

The customer's drawn **signature** is captured on the record's **Completion**
tab. Completing without one records a warning rather than blocking an engineer
whose tablet is offline or whose customer refuses the pad.

## The call-back

One day after a visit is **Completed**, HotCRM prompts the supervisor to call
the site and log a **satisfaction rating** — the same loop cases already use,
run on the visit instead of the ticket.

## Who can do what

| Role | Assets | Work orders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Service Agent** (desk + dispatch) | Read and maintain | Raise, dispatch and edit any job |
| **Field Engineer** | Read, and correct what they find wrong on site | See the whole board; edit only the jobs assigned to them. Billing fields are read-only, and dispatch notes are hidden. |
| **Sales Rep / Sales Manager** | Read-only — what a customer already owns is renewal context | No access |
| **System Administrator** | Full control | Full control |

## Not included

Deliberately out of scope for now, and tracked on the requirement record:

- **Automatic engineer selection** — skill matching, route optimisation and
  travel-time-aware scheduling. Dispatch is manual, on a calendar.
- **Multi-visit jobs and multi-engineer crews** — a work order models one visit.
- **Parts inventory** — parts are recorded as text on the completion log; stock
  is not consumed against the product catalogue.
- **Chat-channel intake** (for example a WeChat group bot) — the work order
  records *Chat / IM* as a channel, but connecting a specific messaging platform
  is a customer-specific extension, not part of the standard product.
